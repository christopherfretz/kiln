# Performance: the reference workload under kiln (WP-551)

This report covers three compiles of the reference workload under kiln. It
says where kiln's time goes in each, compares kiln with GCC 16.1 on the
in-tree shapes that model the workload, and proposes follow-up work. It
records measurements made on 2026-11-04 and is not regenerated. The bench
contract is [spec/bench.md](../../spec/bench.md), and the shapes are
generated by `cargo xtask gen-reflect` (S7 and S8 are new in WP-551). Each
follow-up row is a proposal for the supervisor and is not yet in the
backlog.

## Summary

- **No single hot spot.** No kiln function takes more than 8% of a stage
  by self cost. The cost is spread across the whole frontend, so a quick
  win of 10% or more of a stage does not exist and none is included.
  - The biggest common cost is allocator churn: `malloc`/`free` and their
    internals take 11–17% of self cost in every profile.
  - Next come two call-volume hot paths that run 60–190 M times:
    `TypeContext::view` and the redeclaration-chain walk
    (`DeclArena::redecl`, `Ast::first`).
  - Reflection-heavy compiles also pay for the Oven's interpreter dispatch
    loop: 18–25% of self cost.
- **Generator stage (`-freflection`).** This is the constexpr-heavy stage.
  kiln is fast here:
  - The Oven takes 44% of the 200-class generator compile. The rest is
    template instantiation and sema.
  - On the in-tree model (S7), kiln takes 0.31x GCC's time and 0.10x its
    memory at N=40.
  - kiln grows 2.4x in time and 1.7x in memory from N=40 to N=160. GCC
    grows 3.2x and 3.3x.
- **Production stage (`-std=c++20 -O2`).** This compile's growth is
  super-linear: 30.5 s at N=200, 148 s at N=600. The data points to LLVM
  (`opt` and `llc`), not to kiln's frontend. See
  [the production stage's growth](#the-production-stages-super-linear-growth).
  - The kiln++ process does about 12 s of the 30.5 s stage, scales close
    to linearly, and has no quadratic hot spot.
  - The rest is the `opt -O2`/`llc -O2` child processes. They are not in
    the profile, and their cost depends on how much inlining the one large
    binding function triggers.
  - On the in-tree model (S8), LLVM is 75–85% of the -O2 wall time, and
    the reported peak RSS is `llc`'s.
- **Two kiln correctness bugs make the IR handed to LLVM larger than
  clang's.** They are listed first in the follow-ups and affect run-time
  behaviour as well as compile time:
  - `if consteval` is taken as true in trial folds.
  - `noexcept(std::__relocate_a(...))` evaluates to false.

How the numbers were produced:

- **Builds.** All profiles use the same release kiln++ with debug info.
  R1–R3 were supplied. S7 and S8 were run here under
  `valgrind --tool=callgrind` with `ulimit -v 3000000`.
- **Callgrind's call graph is unreliable on this aarch64 host.**
  Inclusive costs come out inconsistent (children cost more than their
  parents, and recursion is folded wrongly). This report therefore uses
  callgrind only for **self cost, line attribution and call counts**,
  which are exact.
- **Phase split.** The phase table gives each function's self cost to a
  pipeline phase in one of two ways:
  - by crate and module path, for kiln functions;
  - by call-count-weighted propagation through the call-count edges, for
    libc and std code such as `malloc`/`free`/`memcpy`. These edges are
    reliable even though the inclusive costs are not.
- **True inclusive cost.** A frame-pointer sampling profiler (ptrace, 150
  Hz, symbolized with the binary's DWARF) produced the inclusive figures
  for S7 and S8 at N=400, and the growth ratios from N=100 to N=400.
- **The production profile R3 covers the kiln++ process only.** At -O2,
  kiln prints textual LLVM IR, pipes it to `opt -passes=default<O2>`, and
  then runs `llc -O2 -filetype=obj`. Those two child processes are not
  traced. Their share is estimated
  [below](#the-production-stages-super-linear-growth) from S8, where the
  three steps were timed separately.

How to read it:

- **Generators.** A generator compile emits only a small driver, so
  codegen is minor.
  - The 200-class generator (R2) is the constexpr workload: the Oven takes
    44%, and half of that is interpreter dispatch.
  - The fixture generator (R1) is dominated by instantiating the
    workload's headers.
- **The production compile (R3) uses no reflection.**
  - Its Oven share (10.7%) is almost all `try_fold` trial folding of
    ordinary expressions: integer folds, narrowing checks, `if`-condition
    truth, and `is_constant_initialized`.
    - About 575 k evaluations.
    - `compile_func` runs 168 k times, which is why bytecode compile (6.3%)
      outweighs execution (1.5%).
  - Codegen is 23%, and half of that is printing the textual IR that
    `opt` then has to parse again.

### What grows faster than N (S8, N=100 to N=400)

From N=100 to N=400, S8's wall time grows 3.85x. These functions grow
faster than that:

| Function | Growth |
|---|--:|
| `fn_corresponds` | 19x |
| `deduce_call_arg` | 18x |
| `pick_prev_function` | 16.6x |
| `act_on_friend` | 14.5x |
| `friend_decl` | 13x (0.93 s, 11.5% at N=400) |
| `param_list` | 11.7x |
| `deduce_ty` | 9.5x |
| `TypeContext::lookup` | 7.4x |

The term behind the friend rows:

- It is quadratic.
- Every instantiation of the `std::tuple` hidden-friend operators checks
  redeclaration against every earlier friend of the same name.
  `pick_prev_function` loops over all candidates
  (`overload/redecl.rs`).
- It belongs to S8's shape and does not occur in the reference workload.
  R3 makes only 20 k `fn_corresponds` calls and 1.5 k `act_on_friend`
  calls, and R2 makes 155 k `fn_corresponds` calls.

Notes on this table:

- **Comparison with clang.** clang 19 compiles an annotation-stripped
  copy of S8 at N=160 in 16.14 s / 704 MB. kiln takes 18.07 s on the same
  file.
- **IR volume is the same as clang's.** At N=160, kiln emits 18521
  definitions and 34.7 MB of IR; clang emits 18475 definitions and
  37.4 MB. The largest function is `bind_all`, the one function that
  registers every class: 3311 instructions from kiln, 3591 from clang.
- **opt is about 20% slower on kiln's IR than on clang's.** On the same
  host, `opt -O2 -time-passes` takes 12.5 s on kiln's IR and 10.1 s on
  clang's. The difference is concentrated in a few passes:

  | Pass | kiln IR | clang IR |
  |---|--:|--:|
  | LoopVectorize | 0.46 s | 0.00 s |
  | IndVarSimplify | 0.27 s | 0.00 s |
  | SLPVectorizer | 0.47 s | 0.17 s |
  | GlobalOpt | 0.76 s | 0.47 s |
  | SimplifyCFG | 0.60 s | 0.41 s |

  The loop passes run because kiln's IR contains loops that clang's does
  not. Two kiln bugs cause them:
  - **`if consteval` / `std::is_constant_evaluated()` is true in runtime
    code** (F1). libstdc++'s `char_traits<char>::length` becomes a loop
    instead of `strlen`. The same goes for `char_traits::copy`, and
    `basic_string::_M_init_local_buf` zero-fills its buffer in a loop.
  - **`noexcept(std::__relocate_a(...))` is false** (F2).
    `vector::_M_realloc_append` takes the element-wise
    copy-and-destroy path (`__do_uninit_copy`, `_Destroy`) instead of
    relocation.
- **Erroneous-value zero-init in C++26.** kiln also emits 2905 `memset`s,
  where clang emits 166, because C++26 mode zero-initializes automatic
  variables (P2795R5, spec/codegen.md §7r). The production stage builds
  with `-std=c++20`, where this is off.

## The production stage's super-linear growth

The production compile goes from 30.5 s / 1.6 GB at N=200 to
148 s / 3.3 GB at N=600. That is 4.85x the time for 3x the classes.

1. **Most of the stage is LLVM, not kiln.**
   - R3 counts 118.5 G Ir for the kiln++ process alone.
   - Native rate: the syntax-only S8 run executes 15.5 G Ir in about
     1.63 s, roughly 9.5 G Ir/s. The production frontend is similar code
     (sema-dominated, with no Oven execution), so the same rate applies.
   - That puts the kiln++ process at about 12.5 s of the 30.5 s.
   - The other ~18 s (about 60%) is `opt` and `llc`, which run one after
     the other on kiln's output.
   - S8 at -O2 has the same split: LLVM is 75–85% of the wall time.
2. **kiln's frontend does not look quadratic.**
   - No function in R3 shows a super-linear signature:
     - The friend-redeclaration term that makes S8's frontend grow is
       absent (20 k calls).
     - The hottest functions are O(1) accessors whose call counts are
       proportional to declarations and instantiations.
   - S8's frontend grows 2.6x from N=40 to N=160 (this is the syntax-only
     scaling row). From N=100 to N=400 it grows 3.85x even with its
     quadratic friend term.
   - S8's IR emission grows 3.66x from N=100 to N=400.
   - If kiln++ scales linearly, it takes about 37 s at N=600. LLVM then
     takes about 110 s, about 6x its N=200 time for 3x the input.
3. **Why LLVM would grow faster than N.**
   - A rendered binding TU has one registration function whose size is
     proportional to N, plus N sets of small template instantiations that
     `opt -O2` inlines into it.
   - In S8, `opt` grows 4.4x and `llc` 4.1x for 4x N. The pass profile
     is flat in N except GlobalOpt and IPSCCP (6x for 4x N), which are
     module-wide passes over a growing global-constant set.
   - The reference TU's registration function is far larger than S8's.
     Inlining into it and the function-level passes that follow, such as
     GVN, instcombine and the register allocator in `llc`, are the
     expected super-linear terms.
   - This is the one claim the in-tree shapes cannot reproduce, because
     S8's `bind_all` stays at about 20 instructions per class.
4. **The peak RSS is `llc`'s.**
   - In every S8 run, the stage's peak equals `llc`'s peak.
   - The reference's 1.6 GB → 3.3 GB is 2x for 3x N. That fits a backend
     that holds the whole module, more than it fits the frontend
     (R3's frontend allocates about 99 M blocks but frees almost all of
     them).
5. **What kiln adds on top of LLVM.**
   - The textual IR round trip: printing is 11% of the frontend, and
     `opt`'s parse of 35 MB of text is 0.9 s at S8 N=160.
   - `opt` is about 20% slower on kiln's IR than on clang's, because of
     F1 and F2 above.
   - These are constant factors. They do not explain the growth.

**Confirming this needs one measurement on the reference TU** (follow-up
P1):

- kiln++ at N=200 and N=600 with the three steps timed separately:
  `-O2 -S -emit-llvm -Xclang -disable-llvm-passes`, then `opt -O2` and
  `llc -O2` on the output.
- The IR size and the instruction count of the largest function.
- `opt -time-passes` at both sizes.
- clang 19 `-O2` on the same TU for comparison.

If the growth is in `opt`'s inliner on the registration function, there
are three ways to address it:

- Split the registration function in the renderer.
- Mark the per-class registration as `noinline`/cold.
- Run kiln's own pipeline at `-O1` for this TU.

None of these is a kiln frontend change.

## Do the in-tree shapes reproduce the supplied profiles?

- **S7 models the 200-class generator compile (R2).**
  - It reproduces R2's top self-cost list: `run_loop` first, then
    allocator, `view`, `redecl`, and the Oven object model
    (`check_storage`, `set_bits`, `store`, `extent_of`).
  - It puts more weight on the object model (35% against 8%) because of
    `std::string`'s small-string union paths.
  - It puts less weight on interpreter dispatch and bytecode compile.
  - It does not model R2's header-driven template instantiation load.
- **S8 models the production compile (R3).**
  - Its first six self-cost functions are the same six as R3's, in nearly the same order.
  - It reproduces R3's template-instantiation dominance: end-of-TU
    instantiation is 91% inclusive.
  - Its IR at -O2 is as large as clang's.
  - It adds a quadratic hidden-friend term that the reference does not
    have.
  - It does not reproduce the reference's super-linear LLVM time.
- **Neither shape models the fixture TU's macro-heavy header
  preprocessing.** R1 has 249 k macro expansions and 7.7 M

## Quick win

None. The brief allows one change of at most 150 lines that is clearly

- The largest single kiln-owned cost is R2's `run_loop` at 18% self.

## `synth_accessor_god` production TU (WP-633)

This is the reference workload's stress slug: one class with a very large
number of accessor member functions, each bound one by one through a
lambda wrapper and a class-template `def` chain. Its production TU is plain
C++20 compiled against libstdc++ 16.1 with `-O2 -DNDEBUG -fPIC
-fvisibility=hidden -c`. The workload's harness measured 263.8 s and
1.08 GB on kiln, against 75.5 s and 2.43 GB for g++ 16.1 (3.5×). Every
other slug is at most 1.02×. The measurements below were taken on
2026-11-05 through the captured harness command. Run-to-run variance on
this host is about ±30%, so each before/after pair was taken back to
back.

### Split of the real TU

"Before" is the harness-profile kiln++ at main bd696efeb. "After" adds the
two WP-633 changes below. The `-c` row runs the driver's own pipeline
(`opt -passes=default<O2>`, then `llc -O2`) through a timing wrapper that
records each tool's wall time and peak RSS.

| Stage | Before wall | Before peak RSS | After wall | After peak RSS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| parse + sema (`-fsyntax-only`) | 8.01 s | 459,948 KB | 9.21 s | 460,620 KB |
| + codegen + IR print (`-emit-llvm -o /dev/null`, no IR passes) | 12.75 s | 1,044,128 KB | 14.07 s | 876,556 KB |
| full `-c` | 334.75 s | 1,078,920 KB | 310.96 s | 1,083,824 KB |
| … of which `opt` | 306.48 s | 702,724 KB | 281.37 s | 702,964 KB |
| … of which `llc` | 15.93 s | 1,078,920 KB | 15.58 s | 1,083,824 KB |

The IR is byte-identical before and after:

- 162,939,973 bytes;
- 35,431 definitions and 18,272 comdats;
- 1,771,581 instructions.

The object file is 17,500,448 bytes. One function holds 92,547 of those
instructions: the TU's registration function, with 17,221 basic blocks,
8,613 invokes, 6,455 landing pads and 10,762 allocas. All the other
functions are under 1,000 instructions.

The kiln frontend is 14 s of the 311 s. The rest is LLVM.

### Where `opt` spends it

`-time-passes-per-run` on the real TU reports 226.4 s of pass time. Two
single pass runs on the registration function account for 91.6% of it:

- one `JumpThreadingPass` run: 52.9%;
- one `MemCpyOptPass` run: 38.7%.

The aggregated `-time-passes` run agrees: JumpThreading is 64.8% and
MemCpyOpt is 29.8%. Both passes are super-linear in the size of one
function with many landing pads and allocas. Smaller functions are not
the problem: every other function together costs about 8%.

The other compilers on the same TU:

- **clang 19:** 432.52 s and 1,345,980 KB in total. Of that, 417.9 s is
  codegen, with JumpThreading at 254.7 s and MemCpyOpt at 123.8 s. On this
  shape kiln's IR costs LLVM no more than clang's own IR does.
- **LLVM 22** `opt`/`llc` on kiln's IR: 412.73 s in total (`opt` 375.40 s,
  `llc` 21.62 s). Time moves from JumpThreading to MemCpyOpt (43.2%), LICM
  (39.9%) and LoopRotate (11.6%).

Neither is faster.

The obvious LLVM limits do not help either. The run used
`-jump-threading-threshold=0 -jump-threading-implication-search-threshold=0
-memssa-check-limit=10` on the after binary and gave these results:

- `-c` total: 293.61 s;
- `opt`: 265.93 s;
- JumpThreading: 158.7 s, 60.8% of pass time;
- MemCpyOpt: 84.2 s, 32.3%.

The cost is not in the duplication or the clobber-walk budgets these flags cap.
It is in the per-function analysis work that both passes repeat over
17 k blocks. Lazy value info and dominator-tree updates are the likely
source, but that is not isolated. Since no pass flag helps, the pipeline was
not changed.

GCC compiles the same TU in 75.5 s. The difference is in the optimizer's
treatment of one huge function: GCC's pass set at `-O2` is linear enough
here, and LLVM's is not. It is not in the amount of work kiln hands the
optimizer.

## WP-644: subobject path cache

F11 above. The walks from an allocation's root to the object an access

### The cache

`Types::hop(walk, t, off, p)` is the walk from record `t` toward the
object of type `p` at byte `off`, up to its first array or union. At a
union it returns a mask of the members that may hold the object, so the
walk reads the active and possibly-active members from memory and tests
mask bits. `Types::extent` and `has_subobject` cache their answer for
the first record below any leading arrays. Each of the three maps holds
at most `PATH_CACHE_MAX` = 16,384 entries and is cleared by the insert
past that.

Hit rates (temporary counters, all three maps together):

| program | hits | misses | clears | entries at exit | footprint |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| S7 (N = 40) | 654,396 | 58 | 0 | 58 | 3,731 bytes |
| S8 | 31,450 | 19 | 0 | 19 | 1,148 bytes |
| wide-struct-store k = 12 | 61,440 | 8,192 | 0 | 8,192 | 0.70 MB |
| wide-struct-store k = 13 | 122,880 | 16,384 | 0 | 16,384 | 1.40 MB |
| wide-struct-store k = 14 | 16,384 | 262,144 | 15 | 16,384 | 1.40 MB |

The store shape has one key per member, so at k = 14 its 16,384 members
plus the other paths exceed the cap and the hop map thrashes. It is
still no slower, because a miss costs the walk it replaces plus one
insert.

The walk functions, plus `Types::hop`, take about 52 M Ir, down from
96.8 M.

### Design note

The target is −25% `run_loop` self Ir on the Oven shapes. For S7, where
`run_loop` is 17.9% of all Ir, −5% wall needs about −28% of `run_loop`.
The candidates, each with the evidence for and against it:

1. **Pre-decoded instructions** (a parallel `Vec<Insn>` of decoded op
   and operands). Against: the pc is an index into `code` for sites,
   step locations, overflow sites, notes (`notes.rs` reads `code[pc+k]`),
   builtin arguments, meta, `exc`, `life` and every frame's `ret_pc`.
   A second instruction space means either rewriting all of those or
   keeping a pc map. A fixed-size `Insn` of the six-operand maximum is 28
   bytes or more against today's average of about 3 words, which is
   roughly ×2.5 bytecode memory. Bytecode is a measurable part of peak
   RSS on S7/S8, so this fails the +2% bound. Rejected.
2. **One fetch window per instruction.** One checked slice read
   `code[pc..pc+8]`, copied into a local `[u32; 8]` that covers the op
   word and the largest operand count (6), so operand words are read
   from the window without a check. This removes most of the "bounds"
   and "Vec" columns for operand words: about 8–10 Ir per instruction,
   15% of `run_loop`. The encoding does not change. Code is padded with
   7 `Nop` words at the end of every compiled unit, so the window always
   fits. When it does not fit (code read mid-compilation by a nested
   evaluation), a cold fallback copies into a zero-padded window. That
   fallback still panics, as `code[pc+k]` does today, if an instruction
   runs past the end. Chosen.
3. **One register-window borrow** (`&mut self.regs[fb..fb+nregs]`).
   Against: every arm that calls a `&mut self` method (`load`, `store`,
   `enter`, every `mem` call) has to end the borrow and take it again,
   and each `reg!` still needs its own bounds check against the
   window's length. That saves the `Vec` reload but not the check: a few
   Ir on `Mov`/`Const` only. Masking the index to make the check
   disappear changes the failure mode of a bad register from a panic to a
   wrong value. Rejected.
4. **Cold arms out of line** (`#[inline(never)]` methods). There are
   about 30 arms of 13 to 43 lines (`Ret`'s frame teardown, `New`,
   `Delete`, `EnumCheck`, `MpFn`, `VCall`, `Placement`, `ZeroInit`,
   `ScopeExit`, `TypeId`, `IsConstEval`, ...). Their slow paths spill
   `pc`/`fb` and raise register pressure across the whole function.
   Moving the bodies of the rarely executed arms, and the fault and
   slow paths of the hot ones, into `#[inline(never)]` methods that take
   `(pc, fb, window, aux)` targets the "unattributed" column and part of
   "fetch" (5–8 Ir). Its effect on LLVM's register allocation cannot be
   predicted, so this step is measured on its own. Chosen as the second
   step.
5. **Superinstructions.** `ICmp+JmpIfNot` (3–7%) and `JmpIfNot+Step`
   (5–9%) are the only pairs that appear in every loop shape. A fused
   `JmpIfNot+Step` must still report a step fault at the `Step`'s pc and
   keep the counters identical. That is possible by fusing in place: the
   `JmpIfNot` arm checks whether the next word is a `Step` and runs it
   inline, so the old sequence stays the encoding and nothing addresses
   a new pc. The saving is one dispatch on 5–9% of instructions, which
   is 3–6% of `run_loop`. Kept in reserve, used only if steps 2 and 4
   miss the target.
6. **A cheaper `Step` check.** `Step` is 6–14% of instructions, but its
   arm is already one add, one compare and one store of the counter.
   Keeping the counters byte-identical leaves almost nothing to remove
   beyond what the window saves. Not pursued on its own.
7. **Threaded code, computed goto, a JIT, `get_unchecked`.** Excluded by
   the brief and by `forbid(unsafe_code)`.

The plan is three commits, each measured with the same cachegrind runs:
(A) the fetch window and the padding, (B) the cold arms out of line, and
(C) the in-place `JmpIfNot+Step` and `ICmp+JmpIfNot` fusion, only if A
and B leave the Oven shapes short of −25%.

- **A, the fetch window** (−7 to −9 Ir). One checked read of
  `[u32; 8]` per instruction (`kbc::window`); every compiled unit is
  padded with 7 `Nop` words so the read always fits.
- **B, cold arms out of line** (−2 to −3 Ir). 48 opcodes moved into
  `#[inline(never)] Vm::run_cold`. With a wildcard arm the gain was under
  0.5%: the `match` then needed a second range check. Naming the cold
  opcodes in one arm keeps a single jump table. `run_loop` shrank from
  51.7 KB to 13.1 KB of machine code (13.6 KB after E; `run_cold` is
  22.0 KB).
- **C, in-place fusion** (−0.7 to −2.6 Ir). `Mov+Mov`,
  `JmpIfNot+Step` and `ICmp+JmpIf`/`ICmp+JmpIfNot`. The encoding does
  not change (spec/oven.md "Dispatch").
- **D, `Step` fusion** (−0.4 to −1.1 Ir). `Step+Mov` and `Step+Const`,
  the commonest pairs on S7 after C's.
- **E, frame borrow** (−0.4 to −0.6 Ir). The pure register arms index
  a local `&mut self.regs[fb..]`, so the `Vec`'s base and length are not
  reloaded after each store. The design note rejected this as worth
  only a few Ir on `Mov`; measured, it is half an Ir, kept because it
  costs nothing.
- **F, padding reclaim** (no Ir change). A put 7 `Nop` words after every
  unit: on S7, 19,867 units × 28 B = 556 KB of stream, +1.1% peak RSS in
  the first A/B. The next unit now truncates the previous unit's padding
  before its first word (`kbc::unpad`), so only the end of the stream is
  padded: 28 bytes in all, against main's stream.
- **G, borrowed window** (+0.1 to +0.8 Ir; −12% wall on
  `churn-vector`). The first A/B found `churn-vector` 2.3% *slower* than
  main at E despite 20% fewer dispatch Ir. Timing each step's binary
  placed the regression at A (main 680 ms, A 715 ms). The copied
  `[u32; 8]` was spilled to the stack (`ldp`/`stp`) and every operand
  read loaded it back, so each operand went through a store-to-load
  forward on the critical path of the next dispatch. `run_loop` now
  borrows the window in place (`kbc::window_ref`), and `run_cold` fetches
  its own instead of taking `run_loop`'s, so no window has its address
  taken; the dispatch head is one `ldr` of the op word from the stream.
  Four arms that call `&mut self` methods hoist their destination index
  first. G costs a few tenths of an Ir (some operand addresses are
  recomputed from the stream pointer) and is kept for the wall time.

`SetActive` and `PushCleanup` (3.4% of S7's instructions). Each of those
arms calls a memory or union walk that costs far more than the call, so
moving them back would gain under 1 Ir and grow `run_loop` again.

### Design note

- **(a) Cheap-first check: taken.** `check_plain` is one predicate:
  `gen == raw >> GEN_SHIFT && live && flags & refuse == 0 && base !=
  UNBACKED && (raw & (OFF_MASK | PAST)) + len <= size`. Keeping the
  past-the-end bit in the offset makes a past-the-end pointer fail the
  bounds compare (bit 32 exceeds every size), so it needs no test of
  its own. Null is frame slot 0, which is never live. The
  function-pointer slot is refused by name. `refuse` always holds
  `EXTERNAL`, and every allocation whose access depends on its origin
  carries that flag, so the origin test is not needed. A false result
  runs the pre-WP-650 body unchanged (`check_ordered`), so the reported
  fault is still the one the order picks.
  
  No "plain" flag bit was added: all eight `Alloc` flag bits are in
  use, and `flags & refuse` over the existing bits does the same job.
  `Alloc` does not grow. Alone, step 1 was slower (`churn-vector`
  +1.7%): the new branch sat inside two outlined calls. Inlining the
  plain path into every caller (step 2) is what pays.
- **(b) Decode once per access: taken in part.** The plain path no
  longer builds a `Ptr`. It splits the slot bits once to pick the space
  and the allocation vector, and returns `Sp` and the absolute offset to
  the caller. The `Load`/`Store` that follows still calls
  `Memory::space(sp)`, a 2 Ir re-match. Passing `&Space` instead of `Sp`
  would mean changing the signature of every `check` caller in `vm.rs` and `vm/*.rs`, and
  `store` needs the space mutably after `check`'s shared borrow ends.
  Not worth 4 Ir per access.
- **(c) `with_off` in place: not needed.** `Ptr::with_off` already does
  a masked add and sets the past-the-end bit on the raw word, with no
  decode or encode: 6 Ir. `churn-vector`'s 100.6 M is 12 Ir per
  `PtrAdd` over its two calls. The decode and re-encode the brief
  describes are not in it.
- **(d) Bulk `release`: not taken.** `release` clears no shadow
  bitmap. Per released slot it sets `live = false`, tests `HAS_UNION`
  and pushes the slot onto the free list, about 18 Ir. That is 0.38% of
  S7 and 1.8% of `vector-alloc`. No range primitive applies.
