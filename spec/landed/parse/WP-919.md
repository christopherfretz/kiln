# WP-919 — frontend throughput on large nested template-id TUs

WP-879 measured kiln's `-fsyntax-only` time on a generated TU of
reflection-keyed, repeated nested template-ids. kiln was linear in the source
size but about 3x steeper than g++-16, at a tenth of its memory. This WP
profiled the slope, removed it, and keeps it tracked. Numbers, commands and
the real-TU comparison are in docs/status/perf-history.md "WP-919".

None of the brief's suspects caused the slope:

- the template-id and specialization lookups were 3%;
- argument comparison was not quadratic;
- nothing was re-lexed.

The cause was repetition in the lookahead. Each component of a nested
template-id was re-scanned by every lookahead predicate before it was parsed.
On dup4 there were about 3M `tok_at` calls: 1.5M from `scan_name_walk_in`,
530k from `decltype_scope_end`, 207k from `template_args_dependent` and 178k
from `splice_end`. Every re-scan classified its names again through
`Sema::classify_name`, and each lookup allocated its result vectors. The
WP-086 template-argument memo was already hitting: 79% of 342k calls on dup6,
with only 1,401 stamp changes. It did not cover the name scans or the
classifications inside them.

## Design (spec/parse.md §2.2, §2.5)

- **Dense keyword table.** `Keywords` is a `Vec<Entry>` indexed by
  `Symbol::index`, holding the `Kw` and the builtin keyword. The keywords are
  interned when the table is built, so it spans exactly the symbols interned
  up to then. A later symbol is out of range and is not a keyword. One
  indexed load replaces the SipHash lookup.
- **Classification memo.** `Sema::classify_name` and
  `Sema::classify_nested_name` memoize by (name, qualifier, nested) in
  `Sema::classify_memo`.
  - The memo is valid while `Sema::lookup_stamp()` is unchanged. The stamp
    is the fingerprint the WP-086 memo already uses: declaration count,
    lookup generation, context, scope-stack shape, prototype parameters and
    lambda init-captures. A different stamp empties the memo.
  - Classification is side-effect free, so a hit answers as the lookup would.
  - At most 4,096 entries are kept. A full table is emptied, so memory stays
    bounded.
- **Name-scan memo.** `Parser::scan_name_walk(i)` memoizes by token index in
  the WP-086 memo (`TemplateArgsMemo::names`).
  - It uses the same stamp and token-stream rules: replays, injected
    semicolons and `split_greater` empty it.
  - It is skipped while a decltype qualifier is pending, because the walk
    reads it, and after the stack guard halts the parse.
  - It holds at most 4,096 entries.
- **A stronger stamp.** `Sema::lookup_stamp` also hashes each scope's last
  local and its using-declarator count. By counts alone, the reinstated
  declaration scopes of two function templates with alike parameter lists
  matched, so the classify memo answered one's `U` with the other's. The
  libc++ 19 `tuple` `noexcept(_And<is_nothrow_constructible<_Tp, _Up>...>)`
  hit this; it is pinned by
  `tests/conformance/except.spec/deferred-noexcept-alike-templates.cpp`.
- **`decltype_scope_end`** reads the token once and dispatches on its keyword.
  A plain identifier goes straight to the pack-index check, and only a
  non-keyword, non-identifier token is tried as a splice.
- **`TokenSource::get`** has an inlined fast path for an index inside the
  buffered window. The rest is `get_slow`.

All output is unchanged: diagnostics, IR and mangling. The corpus, the
libc++ M3 gate, and before/after diffs of the real TUs' stderr and dup6's IR
check this.

## Results

dup8 `-fsyntax-only`, interleaved best of 5 on the same loaded host:

- before: 1.20 s, 3.16x g++-16's 0.38 s;
- after: 0.45 s, 1.18x g++-16, with RSS unchanged at 17 MB (g++-16: 181 MB).

The target was at most 1.5x. The real TUs got 2-10% faster:

- fmt: 0.80x g++-16
- nlohmann_json: 0.96x g++-16
- range-v3: 0.91x g++-16
- Boost.Hana: 0.66x g++-16

## Guard

`bench/frontend/nested-template-ids-dup.cpp` is a header-only entry with
`generate: nested-dup 200 6 4`. The harness generates dup6 (1.3 MB) into the
run's scratch directory at bench time (spec/bench.md "Generated entries";
`xtask/src/bench/generate.rs`, pinned to the Python generator's bytes by a
unit test), so the slope is tracked without committing a megabyte of source.

## Remaining hotspots (follow-ups)

- `lookup_stamp` is 6% (on dup4, 361k calls at about 57 Ir each), hashing the scope
  stack on every memo probe. It could be cached per scope change or passed
  down a scan.
- `tok_at` is 11%: the predicates still walk the same tokens before a
  memo answers. `get_slow` (3.7%) is the read-ahead that pulls tokens from
  the preprocessor, which is inlined into it.
- Lookup allocation (`free`/`malloc` 6.4%): `lookup_name_scoped` and
  `filter` build `Vec` results that a small-vector or a borrowed slice
  would avoid.
