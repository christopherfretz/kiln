# WP-829 — lvalue alignment through a pointer

A WP-819 follow-up. The contract is in spec/codegen.md §3.3 "Pointer
alignment". This file records what landed and what was deferred.

## What changed ([expr.unary.op]/1, [expr.sub]/2, [expr.ref]/3, [expr.mptr.oper]/3)

kiln gave the lvalue of `*p`, of `p[i]` through a pointer and of `p->m`
the pointee type's complete-object alignment. clang 19 instead takes the
pointer's alignment from `EmitPointerWithAlignment`, which kiln already
mirrored in `builtins.rs` `pointer_with_alignment` (WP-819). That helper
now serves these users as well:
- `*p` (`expr.rs` `unary_lvalue`);
- `p[i]` through a pointer, at `alignmentAtOffset` of the constant byte
  offset or at one element for a variable index (`subscript_lvalue`);
- `p->m` and a vector element through `->` (`member_lvalue`,
  `ext_vector_lvalue`);
- the object of `p->*m` (`memptr.rs` `mp_object`);
- the object of a member call through `->` (`class.rs`);
- the operand of a scalar derived-to-base conversion
  (`emit_class_ptr_cast`) and of `dynamic_cast`, whose vtable pointer
  load takes that alignment.

The old rule was wrong in two ways:
- **Under-aligned** (slower, not wrong): `*&x.m`, `(&arr[0])[4]` on an
  `alignas(64)` array, `*(int *)&s` and `(&b->v)[0]` lost the known
  alignment.
- **Over-aligned** (a miscompile): a class with a virtual base of
  stricter alignment was accessed through a pointer at its
  complete-object alignment. As a base subobject it is only aligned to
  its non-virtual alignment, so at `-O2` LLVM could emit aligned vector
  accesses on a misaligned address (`movaps` on x86-64).

`vbase_align` (`getVBaseAlignment`) also compared the derived pointer's
alignment with the derived class's complete-object alignment; clang's
`getDynamicOffsetAlignment` uses its non-virtual alignment, so
`p->v` through an `NV *` with a stricter virtual base now gets the
virtual base's alignment as in clang.

## Tests

- `tests/codegen/lvalue_pointer_alignment.cpp`: byte-exact against clang
  19 on both triples. It covers `alignas` structs and members, an
  over-aligned array with constant and variable indexes, classes with
  virtual bases through a pointer, `this` and a reference, member
  pointers, casts through `char *`/`void *`, `packed` and
  `#pragma pack` structs, a decayed `alignas` local buffer,
  `__builtin_assume_aligned`, a vector member through `->` and a member
  call through `->`.
- `tests/conformance/class.mi/virtual-base-subobject-alignment-run.cpp`:
  an `-O2` run test copying four doubles through a base subobject
  pointer at offset 8. The old code faults on x86-64 (`movaps`);
  AArch64 tolerates the misaligned access, so it passed there either
  way.

## Measurement

On two libc++-heavy TUs (containers, strings, streams, `function`,
`variant`, `shared_ptr`; then atomics, `any`, `deque`, `list`, `span`,
ranges and `alignas` types), at `-fclang-compat=19 -O0` against
`clang++-19 -O0`: 1044 common functions and 5443 loads and stores. There
were 0 `align` differences both before and after. libc++ was already
at parity, because it does not hit these patterns at `-O0`. The IR-diff
corpus is unchanged at oracle 19 (1080/1114 exact, 34 skipped).

## Deferred

- A `delete` expression's operand still has the natural pointee
  alignment (`new_delete.rs` `emit_cxx_delete`; clang uses
  `EmitPointerWithAlignment`). Only the array-cookie and vtable loads
  are affected.
