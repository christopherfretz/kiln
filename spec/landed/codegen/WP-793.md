# WP-793 — outline the elements of a large expansion statement

## Symptom

One TU of the reference workload takes 437 s at `-O3 -c` under kiln
(frontend 32 s, `opt default<O3>` 371 s, `llc` 11 s) and 150 s under
g++ 16.1. WP-783 (spec/landed/codegen/WP-783.md) showed that the time
is LLVM's on one function template, whose body is a large `template for`
([stmt.expand], P1306R5), and that kiln's pre-opt IR for that source
matches clang's. clang cannot compile the TU, so the measurements below
use a scrubbed IR closure of it: the function's pre-opt IR together
with every function it transitively calls, with names and strings
anonymized. `opt-19 -passes='default<O3>' -time-passes` on the closure:

| pass | time |
|---|---:|
| GVN | 184.9 s (50 %) |
| LoopFullUnroll | 142.3 s (38 %) |
| IndVarSimplify | 28.7 s (8 %) |
| total | 370.4 s, 345 MB peak RSS |

The function without its callees optimizes in 3 s, so inlining is
load-bearing. The first GVN run, before full unrolling, costs about 6 s.
The rest is the second GVN run, after the inliner (about 13,000 inlines
into the function) and LoopFullUnroll have grown it.

## Findings

**Loops.** The function has 307 top-level loops. Of them, 286 are
range-`for` loops over constexpr spans of constexpr
tables. Their trip counts are compile-time constants, so LoopFullUnroll
expands them, together with the linear searches that the inlined
predicates bring in. The loops are clang's loops: kiln's pre-opt IR for
std-only twins (a range-`for` over a constexpr `std::span`,
`char_traits` length/compare/find, string construction from literals,
constexpr/inline/template helpers) matches clang 19's, and no
kiln-specific lowering leaves an extra loop.

**GVN.** GVN's cost is superlinear in the size of the one function it
runs on (MemoryDependence queries over a function of tens of thousands
of instructions after unrolling). No IR shape kiln emits and clang would
not was found in the hot function. A synthetic `template for` TU
(below) reproduces the growth: doubling the element count from 200 to
400 raises GVN from 11.6 s to 49.5 s.

**InstCombine fixpoint.** `function(sroa,early-cse<memssa>,simplifycfg,instcombine)`
reports "Instruction Combining did not reach a fixpoint after 1
iterations" on the closure. This is LLVM 19 behaviour, not a kiln
pattern: clang 19 and clang 22 report it on a 15-line std-free TU (two
loops over an array member searched by index, one guarded by the
other's result), on clang's own IR. `default<ON>` does not verify the
fixpoint, so it costs nothing in a real build. No kiln change.

**Why WP-641 did not apply.** WP-641 (spec/landed/codegen/WP-641.md)
outlines runs of at least 256 consecutive expression statements of one
compound statement (`Cg::emit_stmt_list`). `emit_expansion` emits each
element of an expansion statement as its own compound statement, so
`emit_stmt_list` never sees the expansion as one list. Each element has
a few dozen statements, far below `MIN_STMTS`, and they are
declarations, `if` statements and range-`for` loops, which WP-641 does
not outline. Expansion elements need their own rule, below, which
reuses WP-641's helper machinery.

## Rule

While optimizing (`-O1` and above), an expanded expansion statement
with at least two elements whose sizes total at least
`-fkiln-outline-expansions=N` (default 2048,
`kiln_codegen::OUTLINE_EXPANSIONS_DEFAULT`; `0` disables) emits each
element of at least 16 (`outline::MIN_ELEMENT`) as an internal helper
called in its place (`Cg::expansion_plan`, `Cg::emit_outlined_element`,
crates/kiln_codegen/src/outline.rs). The size of an element is its
statements plus the AST nodes of its expressions. The init-statement and
the prelude (`__range`, `__begin`, and the structured-binding object of
the destructuring form) stay in the parent. The function-level
exclusions of WP-641 apply (coroutines, constructors and destructors,
`optnone`, naked, live VLA bounds and so on).

An element stays inline when it contains:

- a label, `goto` or computed `goto`;
- a `case` or `default` label of a `switch` outside the element;
- a coroutine statement, `asm`, or a contract assertion;
- an expansion statement that is not expanded;
- `return` of a named return value (its destruction is guarded by a
  flag in the caller);
- an expression WP-641 excludes (a statement expression, label address,
  `co_await`, splice, a call of a frame-bound builtin or of a function
  that returns twice).

`break` or `continue` of a loop or `switch` inside the element, nested
expansion statements, `try` blocks and lambdas are allowed.

**The helper.** `<parent>.expand.<n>`, where n counts from 0 per
function. The helpers of an expansion statement nested in an outlined
element are named after the helper that contains them
(`<parent>.expand.0.expand.0`). Its linkage, parameters and attributes follow
WP-641: one `ptr nonnull noundef` per parent local the element names, in
first-reference order, then `this`, then the closure object. It is
`noinline`, `nounwind` exactly when nothing in it can throw, and
`noreturn` when no path returns, in which case the call is followed by
`unreachable`. The element's own locals, temporaries, cleanups and
landing pads live in the helper. An exception leaves the helper after
the element's locals are destroyed and reaches the call, which unwinds
to the parent's live cleanups. So destruction order is the inline order
([except.ctor]/1-2, [stmt.expand]/2: each element is its own compound
statement).

**Leaving an element.** A helper whose element has no `break` or
`return` of the expansion statement returns `void`. Otherwise it returns
`i32`:

- `0` for falling off the end of the element, or `continue`;
- `1` for `break`;
- `2` for `return`.

Each exit runs the element's cleanups first. The parent switches on the
status: `1` branches through the parent's cleanups to the block after
the expansion statement, and `2` branches to the parent's return path.
If the element can return, the parent passes its return slot as the last
argument, and the helper stores the returned value there, using the
parent's return convention (scalar, coerced or `sret`). A `void`
function passes no slot.

Below the threshold, and at `-O0`, the IR is byte-identical to the
emission before WP-793.

**Threshold.** Calibrated with the synthetic of
`bench/frontend/expansion-outline-obj.cpp` at E elements (about 156 AST
nodes each), `opt-19 default<O3>` wall time and peak RSS of the
function emitted inline and outlined (`-fkiln-outline-expansions=0` and
`=1`):

| E | nodes | inline | outlined |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 1,248 | 0.05 s, 66 MB | 0.05 s, 65 MB |
| 16 | 2,496 | 0.11 s, 67 MB | 0.08 s, 65 MB |
| 32 | 4,992 | 0.32 s, 74 MB | 0.14 s, 66 MB |
| 64 | 9,984 | 1.19 s, 96 MB | 0.25 s, 68 MB |
| 100 | 15,600 | 2.88 s, 137 MB | 0.39 s, 69 MB |
| 200 | 31,200 | 15.8 s, 325 MB | 0.75 s, 75 MB |
| 400 | 62,400 | 69.4 s, 1,036 MB | 1.61 s, 85 MB |

Inline and outlined are equal at about 1,000 nodes, and outlining wins
from about 2,000. 2048 keeps every expansion statement in the corpus
except deliberately large ones on the old path. 16 keeps a trivial
element (a single call or assignment) inline, where a call would cost
more than the body.

## The reference workload

The fix cannot be run on the reference workload's source here. Its
effect was measured on the closure by applying the equivalent IR change
by hand. The large function was cut at its single-entry, single-exit
points: points that no loop, branch or cleanup path crosses, except the
function-level unwind path. This gives 260 regions of about 290
instructions each. Each region, with the landing pads only it reaches,
was extracted into an internal `noinline` function (LLVM's
CodeExtractor, `opt -passes=extract-blocks`). 258 of the 260 extracted.
The parent shrank from 132,848 lines to 12,680. `opt-19 default<O3>
-time-passes` on the result:

| pass | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| GVN | 184.9 s | 0.72 s |
| LoopFullUnroll | 142.3 s | 0.46 s |
| IndVarSimplify | 28.7 s | 0.34 s |
| total `opt` | 370.4 s | 8.3 s (9.7 s wall) |
| peak RSS | 345 MB | 231 MB |

`llc-19 -O3` on the optimized result takes 6.9 s. The projection for
the TU at `-O3 -c` is therefore about 32 s + 9 s + 11 s ≈ 55 s, against
437 s before the change and 150 s under g++ 16.1. The reference
workload's own re-timing confirms the number once this lands.

## Tests

- `tests/conformance/stmt.expand/outline-run-O2.cpp` (`-O2
  -fkiln-outline-expansions=1` run test, `clang>=23`). Each case runs
  the same body as a `template for` and as a `for` loop, and compares
  event logs, results and exceptions over a range of inputs. The cases
  cover:
  - `break`, `continue` and `return`, including from nested loops,
    `switch` statements and a nested expansion statement;
  - an `sret` result, a named return value declared inside the element,
    and a prvalue return;
  - parent locals, a reference parameter, a structured binding, a `[&]`
    lambda, `this`, and `try`/`catch` inside an element;
  - the destructuring form inside a lambda.

  It passes unchanged with `=0`.
- `kiln_driver` test `expansion_outlining_shape`
  (crates/kiln_driver/tests/codegen.rs) checks the helpers' names and
  `i32`/`void` results and the parent's status switches. It also checks
  that the IR is byte-identical with the flag at its default below the
  threshold and at `=0`, and that nothing is outlined at `-O0`.
- `kiln_driver` unit test `outline_expansions` covers the flag's
  parsing.
- `bench/frontend/expansion-outline-obj.cpp` (`mode: obj`, `-O3`) is the
  400-element synthetic. It is recorded in `bench/baselines/frontend.json`.
